# Angle Lenses (v1) — how the Prism splits your idea

Six ways to bend your idea into a fresher delivery — the bands the SPECTRUM step produces. These are universal angle *types*; they work for any audience. Audience-specificity comes from CALIBRATE, WHITE LIGHT, and FOCUS, not from the lenses themselves.

Each lens takes the saturated framing (the white light mapped in step 2) and asks a different question to bend your idea away from it.

## 1. The Hidden Cost
**Inversion question:** Everyone is celebrating the upside — what is the unpriced downside?
Take a benefit everyone agrees on and surface the cost nobody is putting on the invoice.
*Example: "AI gives marketers huge productivity gains" → "AI is quietly transferring marketing's budget authority to IT, because CEOs trust IT to instrument it."*

## 2. The Second-Order Effect
**Inversion question:** Everyone is discussing the direct effect — what happens two steps out?
Trace the consequence of the consequence. The first-order take is saturated; the second-order one rarely is.

## 3. The Misattributed Hero (or Villain)
**Inversion question:** The narrative credits — or blames — X. Is it actually Y?
Reassign the cause. The thing everyone is pointing at usually is not the real driver.

## 4. The Premature Consensus
**Inversion question:** Everyone "agrees" on this — but is it actually settled?
Find the take that has calcified into conventional wisdom without earning it, and reopen the question honestly. Not "everyone is wrong," but "we don't actually know yet, and here is why that matters."

## 5. The Forgotten Precedent
**Inversion question:** Everyone is treating this as new — when did it happen before?
Find the historical parallel. What does the prior cycle predict that the breathless present-tense coverage is missing? This lens also feeds the "this happened before" story in the brief.

## 6. The Reframe
**Inversion question:** Everyone is solving the stated problem — is the problem itself mislabeled?
Rename the problem. The boldest angle is not a better answer to the question everyone is asking; it is a better question.

---

**Tuning:** Add, cut, or rename lenses to fit your audience or your point of view. The engine uses whatever lenses are in this file. Keep them as angle *types*, not topics.
